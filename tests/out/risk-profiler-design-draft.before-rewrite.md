# Risk profiler – proposed questions and scoring

A proposed client risk questionnaire that gives a rating on the ESMA 1 to 7 scale. The highest rating it gives is 6. It has 8 questions and a date of birth. This draft is for discussion. Every cut-off is a proposal for Compliance to confirm.

_Generated alongside `Risk-Profiler-Proposal.docx`; keep the two in step._

### How the rating is worked out

| Step | What | How | Gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attitude rating | Middle value (median) of the ratings from Q4, Q5 and Q6, and no more than one level above the Q4 rating | 1 to 6 |
| 2 | Time limit | From Q3 | 1, 2, 4, 6 or none |
|  | Capacity limit | From the capacity for loss result below. Low capacity limits the rating to 4; Medium and High have no limit. | 4 or none |
|  | Risk rating | The lowest of step 1, the time limit and the capacity limit, and never above 6. The adviser may lower it, with a recorded reason. When a limit lowers the result, the client is told why. | 1 to 6 |
|  | Capacity for loss | Q1 + Q2 = 2 to 6: Low (2–3), Medium (4), High (5–6). Less than 3 months' savings (Q1), or relying on the money (Q2), cannot reach High. Shown alongside the rating, as Zurich does, and in addition limits the rating when Low (see above), because the client must be able to bear the losses (ESMA para 80). | Low, Medium or High |

### The ESMA scale

| Rating | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volatility | 0–0.5% | 0.5–2% | 2–5% | 5–10% | 10–15% | 15–25% | over 25% |
| Risk | Very low | Low | Low to medium | Medium | Medium to high | High | Very high |

## 2. The questions

### Introduction (shown before Q1)

> **Introduction:** We ask these questions so that your adviser can recommend an investment that suits you. It is our responsibility, not yours, to decide whether an investment is suitable. The questions cover your finances, how long you plan to invest, how you would feel and act if the value of your investment fell, and what you know about investing. The questions about falls in value help us find the level of risk that suits you. Please answer as accurately and fully as you can. If the answers are not accurate and complete, we cannot recommend an investment. Your answers give a risk rating from 1 (very low risk) to 6 (high risk), which your adviser uses, with your other circumstances, to choose suitable funds. Your adviser can help with any question, and will go through the result with you. If your circumstances change, please tell your adviser so that your rating stays up to date.

- *Why we show it:* The guidelines ask firms to explain why the questions are asked, that the firm and not the client decides suitability, and why complete and accurate answers matter (paras 11–12, 23). It also states the human involvement and how the answers are used (para 17). It contains no disclaimer and does not ask the client to confirm that anything is suitable (paras 14–15).
- *Status:* Draft wording, for Compliance to approve.

### Part A – Your finances

**Q1 Financial cushion** – If your income stopped, how long could you cover your normal spending (including any loan or mortgage repayments and support for family) from savings other than this investment?

| Answer | Points |
|---|---|
| 12 months or more | 3 |
| 3 to 11 months | 2 |
| Less than 3 months | 1 |

- *Why we ask:* One question replaces the earlier income, commitments and savings questions. "Normal spending" already includes loan repayments and dependants, and "if your income stopped" tests how secure the income is. It shows what the client could actually fall back on, and it is a fact they can work out, not a self-assessment.
- *Competitors:* Zurich Q8: "Do you have other savings available to you, should you encounter a financial emergency?" (none / some, not earmarked / yes, specific savings). Not asked by Aviva, Standard Life or Irish Life.
- *ESMA:* Liquid assets, regular commitments and source of income (para 37); ask for facts, not whether the client feels they have enough (para 46); family situation (para 24).

**Q2 Impact of a loss** – If this investment lost a large part of its value, what impact would this have on your finances?

| Answer | Points |
|---|---|
| A big impact: I need this money for a specific purpose, and losing part of it would put me in difficulty | 1 |
| Some impact: I would need to make adjustments to my plans, but I could manage | 2 |
| Little or no impact: I have other savings and income to rely on | 3 |

- *Why we ask:* Shows how much a loss would matter to the client. Q1 measures the cash buffer; this measures the effect on their finances. Follows Zurich's structure (big / some / no impact), in our own words: "a large part of its value" replaces "perform poorly", and each answer says what the impact would be.
- *Competitors:* Aviva Q2: "How important to you is the investment you are considering now?" (four levels, from very reliant to surplus funds). Zurich Q9: "If this investment were to perform poorly, what impact would this have on your financial well-being?" (big impact / some impact / no impact). Not asked by Standard Life or Irish Life.
- *ESMA:* Ability to bear losses (paras 12, 80); the overall financial situation (paras 37, 43).

> **Result:** Capacity for loss = Q1 + Q2 (2 to 6): Low (2–3), Medium (4), High (5–6). Each answer is worth 1 to 3, so less than 3 months' savings at Q1, or relying on the money at Q2, gives at most 4: capacity is no higher than Medium without a separate rule. Low capacity limits the risk rating to 4, and the client and adviser are shown why. Aviva and Zurich report capacity or raise a prompt but do not limit the rating; we limit it because the guidelines require the client to be able to bear the losses (ESMA paras 12, 80).

### Part B – Your timing

**Q3 Investment horizon** – How long do you plan to hold this investment?

| Answer | Highest rating allowed |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 year | 1 |
| 1 year to less than 3 years | 2 |
| 3 years to less than 5 years | 4 |
| 5 years or more | No limit (6) |

- *Why we ask:* Higher-risk funds need time to recover from falls. The ESMA scale assumes holding for 5 years or more, so 3 to 5 years is limited to 4 (balanced) and flagged, and 5 years or more has no limit. The question asks about the planned holding period, not when money is first taken out, because some products allow withdrawals along the way.
- *Competitors:* Zurich Q2: "How long do you plan to invest for?" (1 to 15+ years): shorter terms lower the profile, and 5 years or less shows a warning. Aviva: a term slider used only for its charts. Not asked by Standard Life or Irish Life.
- *ESMA:* Holding period and need for liquidity (para 24); effect of time horizon on risk and return (para 80).

### Part C – Your attitude to risk

**Q4 Largest fall you could accept** – Imagine you invest €10,000. What is the largest fall in value over 12 months that you could accept without changing your plans?

_To be built as a slider_ from "No fall" to "40% or more", in 0.5% steps. The € amount on €10,000 is shown as the client moves it. The rating is that of the highest band whose figure is not above the chosen value, so a value between two bands rounds down to the more cautious one. The bands are the same as the earlier list of answers:

| Slider value | Shown as | Points to rating |
|---|---|---|
| 0% to under 2.5% | No fall, or under €250 | 1 |
| 2.5% to under 7% | About €250 to €700 | 2 |
| 7% to under 15% | About €700 to €1,500 | 3 |
| 15% to under 25% | About €1,500 to €2,500 | 4 |
| 25% to under 40% | About €2,500 to €4,000 | 5 |
| 40% or more | About €4,000 or more | 6 |

Marks on the slider at 0%, 2.5%, 7%, 15%, 25% and 40% show where each band starts.

- *Why we ask:* Each band starts at a "bad year" for a fund at that ESMA level: twice the middle of its volatility band. The figures sit between Aviva's and Zurich's (Appendix A). This answer also anchors the attitude rating, which can be at most one level above it. A slider is how Aviva asks it, and lets the client give their own figure instead of picking from a list.
- *Competitors:* Aviva Q5: "Maximum percentage loss tolerable in 12 months?" (slider 0–40%). Zurich Q5: "How concerned would you be with a short-term (6 months) 10% decline?" and Q6: acceptable range of values for €10,000 after 5 years (worst cases 2% to 37%). Standard Life Q7 / Irish Life Q9: "How much would you be willing to place in an investment with possible high returns but a similar chance of losing some of your money?"
- *ESMA:* The model question: what loss over a given period the client would accept (para 46); use concrete figures (paras 31, 48).

**Q5 Reaction to a fall** – Six months after you invest €10,000, markets fall and your investment is worth €8,000. What would you most likely do?

| Answer | Points to rating |
|---|---|
| Sell everything | 2 |
| Sell part, to limit further losses | 3 |
| Keep it and wait for a recovery | 4 |
| Keep it and invest more | 6 |

- *Why we ask:* What the client would do, not how they feel. Holding through a 20% fall fits level 4.
- *Competitors:* Irish Life Q7: "Six months after making an investment the financial markets start to perform badly … What would your reaction be?" (move to secure / wait / invest more). Zurich Q7: "If you could increase the value of your investment by taking more risk, would you …". Not asked by Aviva.
- *ESMA:* Ask about personal choices when facing a loss, with figures (para 48); assess the client's response to loss scenarios (para 31).

**Q6 Objective** – What do you want from this investment?

| Answer | Points to rating |
|---|---|
| To keep my money safe: I want to get back at least what I put in | 1 |
| A return a little better than a deposit account, with very little risk | 2 |
| Steady growth over the long term, accepting that the value will go up and down | 4 |
| Strong growth over the long term, accepting large rises and falls in value | 5 |
| The highest return possible, accepting a high level of risk to get it | 6 |

- *Why we ask:* The same five steps that Aviva and Zurich use, in our own words. Set against Q4 and Q5, it shows up a cautious attitude with ambitious objectives, or the reverse.
- *Competitors:* Aviva Q4: "What risk and return expectations do you have?" (the same five levels). Zurich Q4: "What is your investment objective?" (protect capital … maximise returns regardless of risk). Standard Life / Irish Life: statements such as "I want my investment money to be safe even if it means lower returns".
- *ESMA:* Investment objectives must be collected (paras 12, 20); contradiction between a prudent profile and ambitious objectives (para 51).

> **Result:** Attitude rating = the middle value of the three ratings from Q4, Q5 and Q6, but no more than one level above the Q4 rating, so the loss answer anchors the result, as it does at Aviva. The highest rating given is 6.

### Part D – Your knowledge and experience

**Q7 Knowledge and experience** – Which statement best describes your investment knowledge and experience?

| Answer | Knowledge level |
|---|---|
| I have not invested before, other than in deposits or savings accounts | Low |
| I have a pension or an investment, but I have not followed how its value changes or what it invests in | Low |
| In the last 5 years I have held funds, shares or bonds. I have seen their value fall as well as rise, and I know that spreading money across many investments reduces risk but does not remove it | Medium |
| I invest regularly in a range of funds, shares or bonds, and I understand how risk, return, time horizon and charges affect them | High |
| I work or have worked in investments or financial services, or hold a finance qualification, and I invest regularly | High |

- *Why we ask:* Combines Aviva's experience question and Zurich's knowledge question. Each answer describes what the client has done and how recently, not how they rate themselves. It does not change the rating: low knowledge with a rating of 5 or more is flagged for the adviser.
- *Competitors:* Aviva Q1: "How experienced an investor are you?" (not experienced … made many investments). Zurich Q3: "How would you describe your investment knowledge?" (Expert / High / Moderate / Little / None, each with a definition). Not asked by Standard Life or Irish Life.
- *ESMA:* Ask what the client has done and how recently, avoid self-assessment and tick-lists (paras 46, 47); little knowledge with an aggressive attitude (para 51). Q8 checks the answer with a multiple-choice question.

**Q8 Knowledge check** – Put these four investments in order, from the one with the lowest risk of losing money to the one with the highest.

| Investment | Right position |
|---|---|
| A savings deposit | 1 (lowest risk) |
| Government bonds | 2 |
| A fund holding shares in many different companies | 3 |
| Shares in a single company | 4 (highest risk) |

_Built as_ four items the client drags into order (or numbers 1 to 4), shown in a mixed starting order. There is also an "I don't know" box.

| Result | When |
|---|---|
| Correct | All four in the right order |
| Wrong | Any other order, or "I don't know" |

- *Why we ask:* Checks the Q7 answer with a question that has a right answer, and tests only basic understanding: that cash is safest, that shares carry more risk than bonds, and that spreading money across many companies is less risky than holding one. No calculation is needed, and there is no guarantee-style wrong answer to spot. Afterwards the client is shown the right order and why.
- *Competitors:* None of the four tests knowledge with a right-or-wrong question. Aviva and Zurich rely on the client's own description (Aviva Q1, Zurich Q3); Standard Life and Irish Life do not assess knowledge. The Allianz risk profile site asks clients to rate cash, bonds and shares as low or high risk.
- *ESMA:* Test real knowledge with a question that has a right answer instead of self-assessment (para 46); guard against clients overestimating their knowledge (para 52); understanding of risk, risk and return, and concentration (paras 23, 31).

> **Result:** Knowledge level = from Q7. If Q8 is wrong or "I don't know", the level drops by one (High to Medium, Medium to Low). It does not change the rating: Low knowledge with a rating of 5 or more is flagged for the adviser.

### About you – Date of birth (not a scored question)

**DOB Age** – Date of birth

| Answer | Effect |
|---|---|
| Under 65 | None |
| 65 or over | Flag for the adviser if the attitude rating is 5 or more |

- *Why we ask:* Age does not change the rating. The time horizon (Q5) already does that work, and older clients with a high rating are flagged for the adviser.
- *Competitors:* Zurich lowers the profile gradually with age (by about half a level at 70 and over) and raises it for the young. Aviva, Standard Life and Irish Life do not use age; Standard Life's adviser guide says attitude to risk does not depend on age.
- *ESMA:* Age matters for objectives and holding period (para 24); more care for potentially vulnerable older clients (para 40); retirement as a trigger to review (para 55).

- **Method:** Each client was described once (finances, horizon, the largest fall they would accept, how they would react, objective, knowledge) and the description was translated into each tool's answers. Aviva's result comes from its published scoring code. Zurich's comes from submitting the answers to its live tool on 15/10/2026. The translation between tools is our own judgement, so a one-level difference can come from that. Q1 (cushion) was taken from each client's savings and Q2 (impact of a loss) from how much they rely on the money; these are also the answers given to Zurich's two capacity questions. Re-run on 19/10/2026 for the 8-question version: only our capacity column changed (P16); no rating or flag changed. The Aviva and Zurich results are those obtained earlier.
- **Overall:** The proposed rating is within one level of Zurich for 17 of 18 clients, and agrees with each competitor more often than the two competitors agree with each other. The one exception to both is P7, where the capacity limit gives 4 against 6 at both competitors.
- **Zurich:** Zurich is one level lower for six clients (P2, P10, P11, P13, P14, P16), mostly because it deducts for age over 50, terms under 10 years and little knowledge; we use those as limits or flags instead. It is one higher for three (P6, P17, P18).
- **Aviva:** Aviva ignores the time horizon (higher for P8 and P17), never goes below 3 except for its no-risk 0 (P1, P15), and stops at 6. The three clients more than one level from Aviva are P7 (our capacity limit), P15 (its no-risk 0) and P17 (its horizon is ignored).
- **Changes after the first run:** The loss answer now anchors the attitude rating (at most one level above Q4): P18, who would accept only a 2.5% fall, moved from 6 to 3, the same as Aviva. Relying on the money (now Q2) caps capacity at Medium: P13 moved from High to Medium, the same as Zurich. No other results changed. **Capacity limit (added 19/10/2026):** Low capacity now limits the rating to 4. Only P7 changed, from 6 to 4; P16 is Low but already rated 4.
- **Capacity:** Our capacity matches Zurich's for 13 of 18. Where it differs, ours is one step higher (P2, P4, P11, P15, P18): each of these has a top and a middle answer (3 + 2), which we call High (5–6) and Zurich calls Medium. Zurich gives High only for two top answers. Setting our bands to Low 2–3, Medium 4–5, High 6 would match Zurich for all 18; this is an open decision.
- **Knowledge check:** Clients with low knowledge were assumed to get Q8 wrong. That changed no ratings, because knowledge only raises flags.

## 8. Decisions
### Taken

- Highest rating: 6, as Aviva. Level 7 is not offered.
- Age: a flag only (65 or over with an attitude rating of 5 or more). With 6 as the highest rating, the earlier 70+ limit had no effect, so it is removed.
- Capacity for loss: a separate result (Low, Medium, High). Low capacity limits the rating to 4 (the figure is for Compliance to confirm). Under 3 months' savings, or relying on the money, cannot reach High (built into the two-question score).
- Attitude: the loss answer (Q4, a slider) anchors the rating, which can be at most one level above it.
- Knowledge: a description (Q7) and an ordering check (Q8, ranking four investments by risk); a flag only.
- Loss figures: our own rule (twice the middle of each ESMA band), not Aviva's figures (Appendix A).
- Time horizon: 3 to 5 years is limited to 4 and flagged, in line with Zurich.
- No complex or illiquid products, so no extra questions (to be checked for PRSA, ARF and bonds: Appendix B).
- Vulnerability: no client question. Age and knowledge flags apply at rating 5 or more; anything else is for the adviser's judgement.
- Financial facts: income source, assets and debts are not asked by this tool, as at the competitors. The application form is expected to collect them; this tool is only the suitability section.
- Capacity limit: Low capacity limits the rating to 4. This makes us stricter than Aviva and Zurich for clients like P7; Compliance to be told.
- Updating and re-profiling: the adviser's process. The tool records the date and version of each profile.
- Joint applicants and sustainability preferences: out of scope.

### Still open

- Q4 as a slider: confirm the 0.5% step and the round-down rule with Compliance and the UI design.
- Confirm the loss rule. It uses the middle of each band, which replaces the earlier "top of band" choice: the top would make us more cautious than both Aviva and Zurich.
- Capacity bands: Low 2–3, Medium 4, High 5–6 (as written) or Low 2–3, Medium 4–5, High 6, which matches Zurich for all 18 test clients. Decide which.
- Time limits in Q3 (3 to 5 years: 4; 5 years or more: no limit): check against the recommended holding periods of the funds offered.
- Test the loss figures against our funds' worst 12-month falls and KID stress scenarios.
- Regulatory gaps: see the cross-check in Appendix B.
- Compliance: confirm which rules govern each product. The Consumer Protection Code 2025 (in force 24 March 2026) disapplies its suitability chapter for advice on insurance-based investment products (Reg 11(3)), so the IDD applies to those; whether PRSAs and ARFs are such products is to be confirmed. The ESMA MiFID guidelines are used here as the benchmark.
- Illiquid products: PRSA, ARF and investment bonds may count as illiquid; Compliance to decide, as it affects Q3 and the information needed.

| ESMA level | Volatility band | Middle of band | Our Q4 figure (2 × middle) | Aviva 12-month worst case | Aviva decision table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–0.5% | 0.25% | No fall | none | – |
| 2 | 0.5–2% | 1.25% | 2.5% | 0% | not used |

| 7 | over 25% | – | not offered | 55% | not used |

- **Our rule:** A bad year is a fall of twice a fund's volatility (about 1 year in 40). Q4 uses twice the middle of each ESMA band. The top answer (40% or more) gives 6; level 7 is not offered.
- **Against Aviva and Zurich:** Aviva uses the same rule but takes off its expected return, so its figures are a few points lower. Zurich's 5-year worst cases (11%, 21%, 32%, 37%) are higher. Ours sit between.
- **Why the middle of the band:** The top of each band (20% for level 4, 50% for level 6) would be more cautious than both competitors.
- **Can we stand over it?:** Yes, as a documented convention. Real markets can fall further than this assumes, so test it against our funds' worst 12-month falls and KID stress scenarios, and have Compliance sign it off.
